FIELD REPORT NO. 001 · INITIAL INVESTIGATION

A Visit to the Infamous Kamla Residence

LOCATION: MAHARASHTRA, INDIA · INVESTIGATION 01

The Prabhakar's Haveli. To anyone living outside Maharashtra, it might not strike a chord, but to the people living in the surrounding villages, these three words are enough to send a shiver down their spine. To some it is nothing more than a folktale made up by their parents as a warning to not go venturing into abandoned properties but to others, it is much more than that.

The legend of the Haveli goes back to the 1980s, when Kamla, the newlywed bride of the wealthy Prabhakar family, went "berserk" due to a violent and inexplicable episode, termed as a "demonic possession" by the locals, which allegedly caused her to kill the entire family. Reports say that Kamla suffered immense torment in the days leading up to her manic episode and "snapped". During the investigation, the police found the dismembered bodies of the entire family. All except Kamla. To this date, no one knows what fate befell Kamla, but people who had ventured close enough to the Haveli mention seeing apparitions, and hearing strange noises, from within the empty house.

Our team and I were intrigued by the story presented to us and wanted to check this place out for ourselves before we made any conclusions. On our way to the location, we encountered many locals who were reluctant and borderline hostile when we inquired about the directions to the Haveli. Though it took a while, we reached the location a few hours before dusk. Standing before us was an imposing structure befitting the reputation it has garnered. Despite its battered exterior, there is no doubt that the house had once belonged to a wealthy family. The area surrounding the property was filled with overgrown vegetation.

But out of everything, one thing stood out to us. The door that we expected to be bolted and locked was slightly ajar. It was unusual that the locals would keep the property open given how scared they were of the mention of this name.

The inside of the house was no better. The entire house was littered with broken and overturned furniture covered with dusty sheets and old family photographs coated with dust were decorating the dilapidated walls surrounded by chipped paint. The staircase had a few missing steps, and broken banisters to complete the look. Needless to say, the atmosphere was very thoroughly set.

We set up our equipment and started exploring the house. At first nothing was unusual. The rest of the rooms were in a similar state of destruction. There was little to suggest there had been anyone venturing into the property before us.

However, things began to get a little strange when we started exploring the first floor. While the crew was upstairs, we heard a crashing sound coming from the ground floor. We rushed down to investigate what happened and were promptly shocked to see the Prabhakar family photo, which was previously hanging on the wall, had fallen to the floor. While it initially spooked us, we chalked it up to the deteriorating condition of the Haveli.

However, the incident was enough to unsettle the crew. The atmosphere of the room clearly changed and the rest of the visit was filled with unusual occurrences.

Several members reported hearing footsteps coming from empty rooms. A few others reported hearing sounds that sounded like furniture being dragged across the floor and creaking sounds all around the house. I am a bit reluctant to admit to this, but I distinctly recall hearing something like a woman crying from the higher floors but I don't want to feed into anything. By this point, the crew was uncomfortable with the occurrences so we decided to wrap it up and come back another day.

However, after having our findings reviewed, we found that our visit was more eventful than we anticipated. While the team and I remain skeptical about the rumours and stories around The Prabhakar's Residence, our encounters cannot be entirely dismissed. There are still many unanswered questions, especially about what happened to Kamla. Perhaps there is more to this story and a reason for the villagers' hostility. Whatever it is, I feel like I have missed something important during the visit and I need to find out what.

I think another visit is necessary.

Until next time.

END OF FIELD REPORT NO. 001
